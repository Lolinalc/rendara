If you own a house or apartment for rent in Mérida, you have probably heard both terms. They are often used interchangeably, but they solve different moments in the life of your property.

> **In short:** a **real estate agency** helps you **find** a tenant and sign the lease. A **property manager** takes care of the property **afterwards**, month after month, while it is rented.

## What a real estate agency does

A real estate agency focuses on **marketing** properties. When you want to rent (or sell) your property, it usually:

- Advertises the property on listing sites and to its client network.
- Organizes and handles viewings.
- Screens applicants and reviews their documents.
- Coordinates the signing of the lease.

Its work is concentrated in one moment: **closing the deal**. Once the tenant moves in, its involvement usually ends or drops significantly.

## What a property manager does

A property manager works **for as long as the property is rented**. The goal is for you, the owner, to receive your rent and know how your property is doing without handling the day-to-day. At Rendara, for example, that includes:

- **Collections and finance:** monthly rent collection, deposit tracking, and payment of the property's utilities and taxes.
- **Operations:** tenant support, vendor coordination, maintenance supervision and handling emergencies.
- **Oversight:** periodic inspections, a property condition inventory, and tracking damage and repairs.
- **Administrative follow-up:** lease renewals and follow-up on paperwork.
- **Reporting:** a monthly report so you know what happened with your property and your money.

## The differences at a glance

| | Real estate agency | Property manager |
|---|---|---|
| **When it steps in** | Before renting | After renting |
| **Goal** | Close the deal | Rent arrives and the property is in good shape |
| **Duration** | One-time (a single transaction) | Ongoing (month to month) |
| **Your main contact** | While looking for a tenant | Throughout the lease |
| **Typical tasks** | Marketing, viewings, screening, signing | Collection, maintenance, tenant, reports |

## Which one do you need?

It depends on where your property is right now:

1. **Your property is empty and you want to rent it out.** You first need a **real estate agency** to find a tenant.
2. **Your property is already rented, but it takes up your time.** Collecting rent, answering calls and coordinating repairs is exactly what a **property manager** handles.
3. **You live outside Mérida.** Without someone local to keep an eye on things, any issue gets complicated. A **property manager** is your eyes and hands in the city.
4. **You own several properties or a building.** The more units, the more collections, vendors and follow-up. This is where **professional management** makes the biggest difference.

## Can they work together?

Yes, and it is the most practical setup. This is how Rendara works:

1. A **partner real estate agency** markets your property, selects the tenant and signs the lease.
2. Once the lease is signed, **Rendara takes over the management**: collections, maintenance, tenant support and monthly reports.

Each one does what it does best, and once the property is rented you have a single point of contact.

## Questions to help you decide

Before hiring anyone, ask yourself:

- Does my property already have a tenant, or do I still need to find one?
- How many hours a month do I spend today collecting, answering and fixing things?
- Can I get to the property if there is an emergency?
- Do I know how much my property actually earned last year, after expenses?

If your answers show that your problem starts **after** the property is rented, what you need is a property manager.
